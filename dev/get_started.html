Get started ​

ThermalPhysiology.jl describes how the rates of biological processes, and the time organisms survive, depend on temperature. Inputs and outputs are Unitful.jl quantities.

julia
using Pkg
Pkg.add("ThermalPhysiology")

Three families of models ​

There are three families of models, each with its own function:

FamilyFunctionReturns
Arrheniustemperature_correctiona correction factor, 1 at the reference temperature, or a corrected rate
Thermal performance curvesthermal_performancean absolute or relative rate
Thermal death timesurvival_timethe median time to knockdown at a constant temperature

A model is a struct holding its parameters, built with a keyword constructor. Every model can also be called like a function of temperature:

julia
using ThermalPhysiology, Unitful

arrhenius = ArrheniusModel(0.65u"eV"; T_ref = 20.0u"°C")
temperature_correction(arrhenius, 30.0u"°C")
2.336784505540082

Units ​

Temperatures can be given in any unit, and are converted as needed:

julia
tpc(298.15u"K") == tpc(25.0u"°C")
true

Bare numbers are rejected rather than assumed to be in some unit:

julia
try
    tpc(25.0)
catch err
    err
end
ArgumentError("Thermal death time temperature parameters must carry units, e.g. `39.0u\"°C\"` (got bare value 25.0).")

Properties of a curve ​

The optimum, the critical thermal limits and the breadth of a performance curve are found analytically where possible, and numerically otherwise:

julia
optimal_temperature(tpc), critical_thermal_maximum(tpc)
(30.0 °C, 313.15 K)
julia
q10(tpc, 20.0u"°C")
1.3591409142295225

Plotting ​

The models broadcast over vectors of temperatures, so they are easy to plot, here with CairoMakie:

julia
using CairoMakie

temperatures = collect(0.0:0.25:40.0) .* u"°C"
fig = Figure(size = (700, 400))
ax = Axis(fig[1, 1]; xlabel = "Temperature (°C)", ylabel = "Relative performance")
lines!(ax, ustrip.(u"°C", temperatures), tpc.(temperatures); linewidth = 2)
fig

Thermal death time ​

A thermal death time model gives the survival time at a constant temperature, the temperature that is lethal after a given exposure, and the injury accumulated under a fluctuating temperature:

julia
lethal_temperature(tdt, 120.0u"minute")
37.795880017344075 °C
julia
heatwave = [30.0 + 12.0 * sinpi(h / 24) for h in 0:0.1:24] .* u"°C"
time_to_failure(tdt, heatwave, 6.0u"minute")
366.0 minute

Fluctuating temperatures ​

Because rates are nonlinear in temperature, the mean rate under a fluctuating temperature is not the rate at the mean temperature. The constant_temperature_equivalent (CTE) is the constant temperature with the same mean rate. For example, here the CTE for a fluctuating temperature regime with a mean of 20 °C is:

julia
body_temperatures = [20.0 + 8.0 * sinpi(2h / 24) for h in 0:23] .* u"°C"
uconvert(u"°C", constant_temperature_equivalent(arrhenius, body_temperatures))
21.254968464722822 °C

Fitting ​

Models can be fitted to data, for example a performance curve to measured rates:

julia
temperatures = [5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40] .* u"°C"
rates = [0.05, 0.2, 0.4, 0.62, 0.85, 1.0, 0.72, 0.0]
fitted = fit_thermal_performance_curve(UniversalTPCModel, temperatures, rates)
UniversalTPCModel{Quantity{Float64, 𝚯, Unitful.FreeUnits{(K,), 𝚯, nothing}}, Float64}(304.5223598485393 K, 8.526848301778763 K, 0.9455014825230785)

See Fitting to data for thermal death time data and tolerance landscapes.
